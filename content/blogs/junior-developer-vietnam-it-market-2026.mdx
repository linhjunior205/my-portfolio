## The paradox

If you follow tech news in Vietnam, you will see two headlines that seem to contradict each other:

- "Companies cannot find enough engineers."
- "Fresh graduates cannot find jobs."

Both are true. Reports from VnExpress, ITviec and recruiters describe the same shape: a **large supply of entry-level developers** and a **thin layer of experienced engineers**. Vietnam produces tens of thousands of IT graduates every year, software exports keep growing, and yet companies complain that the people they really need — engineers who can own a system end to end — are rare.

## What the data says

- **Recovery, but selective.** ITviec's 2025–2026 research describes a market that is recovering after the 2023–2024 slowdown, with most surveyed companies expecting growth in 2026 — but hiring is more careful and requirements are higher.
- **Salaries are stable, not booming.** Median IT salaries reported by ITviec stayed roughly flat between 2024 and 2025, which is very different from the bidding wars of 2021–2022.
- **IT is still a top-paying field.** Even in a tougher market, reports such as TopCV's continue to rank IT among the best-paid career paths in Vietnam, with AI and specialized roles the hardest to fill.
- **AI is changing the junior role.** Several employers say AI tools now handle tasks that used to be given to juniors — sample code, simple bug fixes, writing test cases — so they hire fewer juniors and expect more from each one.

Note: many of these numbers come from recruiter reports and surveys, so treat them as signals rather than exact measurements.

## What "junior" means in 2026

The bar moved. A few years ago, a junior was expected to write code from a clear ticket. Today, many teams expect a junior to:

- Ship a small feature **end to end**: API, database change, UI and tests.
- Use AI tools productively **and** catch their mistakes.
- Read an unfamiliar codebase and find where a change belongs.
- Communicate clearly in written English (tickets, PRs, docs).

In other words: the job looks more like "junior engineer who owns small things" and less like "person who types the code".

## How to stand out

### 1. Build real, deployed projects

A deployed app with real users — even a small one — beats ten tutorial clones. Show the full lifecycle: requirements, database design, auth, deployment, monitoring. Put the link and a short write-up on your portfolio.

### 2. Go deep on one stack

Being "okay" at ten technologies is less valuable than being genuinely good at one stack. For web, a stack like **Next.js + NestJS + PostgreSQL** is in high demand. Know how it works under the hood: request lifecycle, ORM queries, indexes, caching.

### 3. Learn to work with AI, not against it

Use coding agents daily, but treat their output like a teammate's pull request. Be the person who can say "this query will be slow without an index" or "this endpoint is missing an authorization check". That judgment is exactly what employers worry juniors lack.

### 4. Get good at testing and debugging

Writing tests and reading stack traces are underrated junior superpowers. They make you trustworthy, and they are the skills that make AI-generated code safe to merge.

### 5. Add a "business" skill

Working as a developer, business analyst and tester on a small outsourcing team taught me that **understanding requirements** is often the hardest part of a project. Developers who can talk to clients, clarify scope and write acceptance criteria are rare and valuable.

### 6. Improve your English

Vietnam's IT industry is heavily export-oriented. Good written English opens doors to foreign clients, remote roles and much better salaries.

### 7. Write in public

A technical blog (like this one), GitHub contributions or answering questions in communities shows how you think. Recruiters notice.

## Conclusion

The 2026 market is harder for juniors, but it is not closed. Companies still need people — they just want juniors who can learn fast, use AI responsibly, and take ownership. If you focus on real projects, one strong stack, testing and communication, you will be in the group that companies are struggling to find, not the group they are filtering out.
